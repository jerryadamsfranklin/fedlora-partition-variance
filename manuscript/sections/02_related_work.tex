\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Variance and seed sensitivity in ML benchmarks.}
Empirical comparisons are sensitive to sources of variation beyond the method under test. Bouthillier et al.~\cite{bouthillier2021accounting} model data sampling, initialization, and hyperparameter choice as variance components in machine-learning benchmarks and show that ignoring them inflates the chance of declaring a false improvement; their recommendations include randomizing multiple sources of variation and accounting for the resulting uncertainty when claiming gains.
% Picard (arXiv:2109.08203): CIFAR-10 range 89.01% to 90.83% over 10,000 seeds (=1.82 pp); ImageNet gap about 0.5%.
Picard~\cite{picard2021seed} isolates the random seed alone: across $10{,}000$ CIFAR-10 trainings the accuracy range ran from $89.01\%$ to $90.83\%$ ($1.82$ percentage points), with about $0.5\%$ on ImageNet. Both lines of work motivate multi-seed summaries and caution around small margins. Neither separates the draw of a federated data partition from the training seed, which is the decomposition this paper targets.

\paragraph{Non-IID simulation in federated learning.}
Federated evaluations routinely induce client heterogeneity by construction.
% Hsu et al. (arXiv:1909.06335): Dirichlet Dir(alpha p); FedAvg degrades as alpha decreases / identicalness falls.
Hsu et al.~\cite{hsu2019measuring} synthesize label skew by drawing each client's class proportions from a Dirichlet distribution $\mathrm{Dir}(\alpha p)$, with smaller $\alpha$ producing more skewed clients, and show that FedAvg accuracy degrades as $\alpha$ decreases.
Natural partitions that follow writers, users, or categories appear in benchmarks such as LEAF~\cite{caldas2018leaf}.
% NIID-Bench (Li et al., ICDE 2022, Sec./abstract finding): six partitioning strategies; no algorithm wins in all cases.
NIID-Bench~\cite{li2022niidbench} compares algorithms under six partitioning strategies and reports that no existing algorithm outperforms the others in all cases.
Federated instruction-tuning work inherits these simulation habits: FedIT~\cite{zhang2024fedit} splits Databricks-dolly-15k into ten shards, one per client, and reports per-client category distributions in which several categories are absent entirely, and FFA-LoRA~\cite{sun2024ffalora} uses a 3-client cross-silo setting with fixed label proportions (for example $[0.1,0.9]$ and $[0.9,0.05,0.05]$), not a Dirichlet draw, on RoBERTa/GLUE and LLaMA/GSM-8K rather than Dolly. None of the evaluations above resamples the partition seed; the single draw is treated as the non-IID condition, even though that draw determines which clients are empty of a category or too small to take an optimizer step.

\paragraph{Federated LoRA methods and evaluation practice.}
FedIT~\cite{zhang2024fedit} aggregates LoRA adapters with FedAvg for instruction tuning.
% FFA-LoRA Table 1 (non-private): MNLI-m 85.05+/-1.1 vs LoRA 82.03+/-10.7; QQP 84.35+/-0.6 vs 83.51+/-3.3; caption: 20 runs mean+/-SD.
FFA-LoRA~\cite{sun2024ffalora} freezes $A$ and trains $B$, reporting accuracies over $20$ runs as mean and standard deviation. In the non-private setting the MNLI-matched means are $85.05{\pm}1.1$ (FFA-LoRA) versus $82.03{\pm}10.7$ (LoRA), and the QQP means are $84.35{\pm}0.6$ versus $83.51{\pm}3.3$; the LoRA standard deviation of $10.7$ on MNLI-matched exceeds the mean method gap, which is published evidence that run-to-run variance in federated LoRA can dominate the reported contrast.
% FLoRA Table 1 (Llama, homo, Alpaca MMLU): FedIT 29.41 vs FLoRA 29.85; text: ``marginal''; MT-bench ``at least 0.2''; 10 clients following FedIT non-IID; App. A.2/Table 2: 1--3 rounds, no seed replication; MMLU on 1,444 samples.
FLoRA~\cite{wang2024flora} stacks heterogeneous-rank adapters and compares to FedIT on MMLU and MT-bench with $10$ clients under FedIT's non-IID setting (matching our client count). Several margins are modest: Llama/Alpaca MMLU is $29.85$ versus $29.41$, which the paper calls ``marginal,'' and homogeneous MT-bench gains are described as at least $0.2$. Those runs use only $1$ to $3$ communication rounds with single reported values and no seed replication, and MMLU is scored on a $1{,}444$-sample subset. Published evaluations therefore sometimes report across-run means and standard deviations, yet they do not resample the partition draw or decompose that run variance into partition and training-seed components when the reported margin is on the order of a point or less.

\paragraph{Gap.}
To our knowledge, no prior study decomposes partition-draw variance against training-seed variance for federated LoRA instruction tuning, or tests whether method rankings survive that partition variance. This paper fills that gap under a pre-registered design.
