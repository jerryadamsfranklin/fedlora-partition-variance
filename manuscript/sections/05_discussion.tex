\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The Partition-Draw Reporting Protocol}
A multi-seed mean alone does not answer whether a federated LoRA ranking is stable under the non-IID draw. FFA-LoRA already shows that across-run standard deviations can exceed method gaps~\cite{sun2024ffalora}; our results go one step further by decomposing that variation into a partition component, a partition-by-method interaction, and a residual training-seed component. At Dirichlet $\alpha{=}0.1$, the residual $\hat\sigma^2_E$ is small relative to $\hat\sigma^2_P$ and $\hat\sigma^2_{PM}$ on TinyLlama, so reporting only run seeds understates the uncertainty that matters for ranking. We therefore propose the Partition-Draw Reporting Protocol (hereafter, the protocol):
\begin{enumerate}
\item Report the number of partition draws, and the partition seeds, separately from training seeds.
\item Pair method comparisons on the same partition draws.
\item Report the minimum difference the design can detect, at the design's number of draws.
\end{enumerate}
When the observed gap is near the paired per-draw SD ($0.00282$ on TinyLlama, $0.00770$ on LLaMA-3.2-3B at $p{=}10$), a few-draw evaluation is nearly a coin flip; when the gap is about $0.023$ to $0.031$, three paired draws sufficed here. The six-to-ten-draw reversal at 3B is the same point demonstrated inside the experiment: stopping at six draws would reverse the top two and cut the estimated interaction variance by more than half.

\subsection{What pairing fixes, and what it does not}
Pairing methods on the same partition draws removes the shared partition main effect from the comparison and is the right default for ranking. It does not remove the interaction: methods can still reorder across draws when their responses to a partition differ. That is exactly the FedIT--FLoRA near-tie ($+0.00055$ on TinyLlama, $+0.00067$ on LLaMA-3.2-3B at $p{=}10$), where best-method and full-order flip probabilities coincide because only the top pair moves. Pairing also does not invent power. Detecting a pre-registered $\Delta{=}0.005$ still needs $5$ (TinyLlama) or $21$ (LLaMA-3.2-3B at $p{=}10$) paired draws, and the observed near-tie would need $206$ and more than $1000$. For that near-tie, pairing roughly halves the requirement at 1.1B ($206$ versus $416$ per method unpaired) but at 3B both protocols exceed $1000$. At some larger $\Delta$ the unpaired design needs fewer draws per method, because the interaction term enters the paired difference twice; where $\hat\sigma^2_{PM}$ dominates, pairing on partitions buys little. Pairing is a variance-control device for the shared partition effect, not a substitute for enough independent partitions.

\subsection{Cross-scale reading}
On TinyLlama the partition share is substantial ($0.508$). On LLaMA-3.2-3B at $p{=}10$ the interaction dominates ($0.992$) and the partition main-effect CI includes zero after truncation, so we do not claim a partition main effect at 3B. Cross-scale CIs on the partition share overlap; the two scales are not distinguishable on that margin. The scales also differ in more than size: TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat-v1.0 is instruction-tuned while LLaMA-3.2-3B is a base model, and base weights are float32 versus float16, so cross-scale differences should not be read as pure scale effects. We report the 3B IID floor descriptively only ($2$ degrees of freedom per method).

\subsection{Limitations}
The study uses two model scales only (1.1B and 3B), one dataset (Dolly), two heterogeneity levels at 1.1B ($\alpha{=}0.1$ and $0.5$), three aggregators, $10$ clients with full participation, $15$ rounds with one local epoch, and held-out next-token loss rather than generation benchmarks. Local updates apply only on complete gradient-accumulation blocks, so clients holding fewer samples than one block contribute no update; at $\alpha{=}0.1$ this affected up to two clients per partition, each holding at most $11$ samples. LLaMA production holdouts run in float16; the float32 spot-check bound the difference well below the $0.01$ effect scale, but the primary 3B numbers remain float16. The MixedLM partition component did not identify, so moment estimates are primary. Communication is reported within method only. RQ4 is exploratory and unreplicated at 3B. The release covers results and metadata, not adapter weights.
Two operational deviations are disclosed. The held-out evaluation was amended
mid-campaign to restore float32 for TinyLlama (\texttt{freeze-v3.1}); 83 of 84
extension cells ran under the amended code and one under its predecessor, and 15
LLaMA held-out evaluations were recomputed to a single code path. Separately, 54
extension cells trained on hosts that installed a torch build other than the pinned
2.2.0+cu121 and were retrained on the pinned stack; the originals are retained in the
repository. Comparing the two versions of those cells, the stack shift averages
$-8.5{\times}10^{-8}$ at 1.1B and $+8.4{\times}10^{-4}$ at 3B
($\mathrm{SD}\,3.0{\times}10^{-4}$, $n{=}15$). The 3B shift is larger than the
observed FedIT--FLoRA gap of $0.00067$, which is a concrete instance of the paper's
argument: an unreported component of the measurement apparatus moved the metric by
more than the method difference under test. All reported results use the pinned stack.
Four duplicate complete run directories were resolved by keeping the newer freeze-v3.1 / torch 2.2.0+cu121 timestamp; both copies already matched the pin.

\subsection{No method recommendation}
We make no method recommendation. The separable FFA-LoRA gaps are large and stable; the FedIT--FLoRA gap is not. The contribution is the measurement design and the conditioning of ranking stability on effect size relative to partition-draw noise.
