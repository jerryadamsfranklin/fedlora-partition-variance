\begin{abstract}
Federated LoRA comparisons often rest on a single non-IID partition draw, even when reported method gaps sit near ordinary run-to-run noise. We ask how much of the variation in held-out next-token loss comes from the partition draw, how often few-draw rankings flip, and how many draws a target effect size requires. In a pre-registered design of $204$ runs at two scales (TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat and LLaMA-3.2-3B),
with partition seeds separated from training seeds, we fit a two-way variance decomposition
for FedIT, FFA-LoRA, and FLoRA on Dolly, partitioned by its category labels at Dirichlet
$\alpha{=}0.1$ and $\alpha{=}0.5$. On TinyLlama the partition and interaction shares are $0.508$ and $0.469$ against a residual of $0.023$. On LLaMA-3.2-3B at $p{=}10$ the interaction dominates ($0.992$) and the partition interval includes zero after truncation. Ranking flips concentrate in the near-tied FedIT--FLoRA pair: the three-draw paired flip probability is $0.377$ on TinyLlama and $0.493$ on LLaMA-3.2-3B, while gaps of about $0.023$ never reverse in a single draw. Detecting that observed near-tie at 80\% power needs 206 paired partition draws on
TinyLlama and more than 1000 on LLaMA-3.2-3B; separable pairs need only 3. Extending
the 3B grid from six to ten draws reverses the top-two ordering and grows the
interaction component by a factor of 2.66, so an evaluation stopping at six draws
would report the opposite ranking. Repeating the design at $\alpha{=}0.5$ leaves the
variance composition statistically unchanged while shrinking total variance by about
an order of magnitude. We propose the Partition-Draw Reporting Protocol: (1)~report the number of partition draws, and the partition seeds, separately from training seeds; (2)~pair method comparisons on the same partition draws; (3)~report the minimum difference the design can detect, at the design's number of draws. After first mention we call this ``the protocol.'' We make no method recommendation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
federated learning, low-rank adaptation, non-IID partitioning, experimental variance, reproducibility, large language models
\end{keywords}
