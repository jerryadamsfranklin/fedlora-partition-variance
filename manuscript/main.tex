\title{Partition Draws, Not Aggregators: Decomposing Result Variance in Federated LoRA Fine-Tuning Under Non-IID Splits}

\author{\uppercase{Jerry Adams Franklin}\authorrefmark{1}}

\address[1]{Independent Researcher, Rochester, NY, USA}

\tfootnote{}

\markboth
{Franklin: Partition Draws, Not Aggregators}

\corresp{Corresponding author: Jerry Adams Franklin (e-mail: jerry.adamsf@gmail.com).}

\begin{abstract}
Federated low-rank adaptation comparisons can rest on a single heterogeneous
client partition draw, even when reported method gaps sit near run-to-run noise. We measure how much
held-out loss variation the partition draw causes, how often few-draw rankings flip, and
how many draws a given effect requires. In a pre-registered design of $204$ runs at two
scales (TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat and LLaMA-3.2-3B), with partition seeds separated from
training seeds, we decompose the variance of FedIT, FFA-LoRA, and FLoRA on Dolly with
Dirichlet label skew at $\alpha{=}0.1$ and $\alpha{=}0.5$. On TinyLlama, partition and
interaction shares are $0.508$ and $0.469$ against a $0.023$ residual. On LLaMA-3.2-3B
with ten draws the interaction dominates ($0.992$) and the partition share truncates to
zero. Flips concentrate in the near-tied FedIT--FLoRA pair (three-draw paired flip
probability $0.377$ and $0.493$), while gaps of $0.023$ to $0.031$ never reverse.
Detecting that near-tie at 80\% power needs $206$ paired draws on TinyLlama and more than
$1000$ on LLaMA-3.2-3B; separable pairs need $3$. Extending the LLaMA grid from six to ten
draws reverses the top-two ordering and grows the interaction component $2.66$-fold, so
stopping at six would report the opposite ranking. At $\alpha{=}0.5$ the composition is
statistically unchanged while total variance shrinks by about an order of magnitude. We
propose the Partition-Draw Reporting Protocol: report the number of partition draws and
their seeds separately from training seeds, pair method comparisons on the same draws, and
report the minimum detectable difference. We make no method recommendation.
\end{abstract}
